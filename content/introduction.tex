\section{Research Context}
\label{sec:intro:context}

Learning-based Android malware analysis depends on the observations available to the
classifier. DREBIN uses features from an application's manifest and bytecode
\cite{arp2014drebin}, while MaMaDroid models sequences of abstracted application programming
interface (API) calls recovered through static analysis \cite{onwuzurike2019mamadroid}.
In graph-based detectors, program structure forms part of the input to representation
learning \cite{bilot2024survey}. A systematic review identified 132 studies of deep-learning
approaches to Android malware defence published between 2014 and 2021
\cite{liu2023deeplearning}.

This dissertation examines contextual representation and adversarial evaluation, with Android
malware analysis at the centre. The term \emph{context} covers two sources of information:
relations among program or system elements, and complementary observations combined for one
prediction. \hybroid{} studies
context across static code and recorded network traffic; \hgann{} studies context within
the application package through groups of related methods. Supporting work examines service
relationships in the Internet of Things (IoT). The industrial control studies use
protocol-specific records and multivariate process windows.

Predictive evaluation must account for the population on which a model is tested.
Jordaney et al.\ document changes in malware that make previously trained classifiers
unreliable \cite{jordaney2017transcend}. Pendlebury et al.\ show how temporally inconsistent
partitions and unrepresentative class distributions can inflate reported performance
\cite{pendlebury2019tesseract}. In an assessment of thirty selected security papers,
Arp et al.\ found at least three of ten methodological pitfalls in each paper
\cite{arp2022dos}. These findings motivate explicit sampling and evaluation assumptions.

Related applications also affect the independence of training and test samples. Drebin
contains repackaged applications with duplicated opcode sequences
\cite{irolla2018duplication}. Zhao et al.\ found limited duplication effects for the
supervised binary detectors they tested, but larger effects for unsupervised malware-family
clustering \cite{zhao2021impact}.

Adversarial evaluation concerns deliberate changes to the input of a defended system.
Athalye et al.\ identified obfuscated gradients in seven of nine non-certified white-box
defences accepted at ICLR 2018; their attacks circumvented six completely and one partially
\cite{athalye2018obfuscated}. Carlini et al.\ specified threat-model and evaluation
requirements in 2019 \cite{carlini2019evaluating}. Tramèr et al.\ subsequently circumvented
thirteen defences, almost all of which had attempted adaptive evaluation
\cite{tramer2020adaptive}. The quality of that evaluation depends on how the attack addresses
the particular defence.

For Android, a problem-space attack must produce a valid modified package that retains its
malicious functionality \cite{pierazzi2020intriguing}. The permitted transformations and
their effect on extracted features therefore belong in the evaluation specification.
Predictive comparison of representations and adversarial assessment of a complete system
are the two research themes developed in this dissertation.

\section{Problem Statement and Scope}
\label{sec:intro:problem}

\subsection*{The representation problem}

Static analysis examines an application without executing it and can inspect code outside
the paths exercised by a test harness. Its coverage depends on the available code and the
analysis approximations; obfuscation can hinder recovery \cite{moser2007limits}.
Dynamic analysis records triggered behaviour through instrumentation, as in taint tracking
\cite{enck2014taintdroid}, but depends on the execution paths reached. Within-system studies
have reported benefits from combining static and dynamic observations
\cite{onwuzurike2018family}. Binary and category classification impose different distinctions
on the observations: a feature that separates malware from benign applications need not
separate malicious categories. RQ2 concerns the additional value of fusing program-graph
representations with network-flow features for the same applications, separately for binary
detection and malware-category classification.

A function-call graph relates methods through recovered call sites. A hypergraph can record
a chosen group of methods as one hyperedge \cite{zhou2006learning}. Replacing each group by
pairwise links can lose its identity; a bipartite graph with distinct method and group
vertices preserves the memberships \cite{agarwal2006higherorder}. Prior Android work
constructs hyperedges from call neighbourhoods and shared permissions
\cite{zhang2023android}, and hypergraph attention already provides a mechanism for learned
membership weighting \cite{bai2021hypergraphattention}. Group construction determines which
methods can exchange information through a hyperedge; membership weights then control their
influence within that structure. RQ3 examines alternative
program-related groups and attention-weighted aggregation against pairwise graph and
non-attentive hypergraph configurations. The distinction between the information retained
by a representation and the operations applied to it is developed in
Section~\ref{sec:background:graphs:hypergraphs}.

Networked and industrial systems require representations suited to other observation units.
A service-communication model must accommodate new legitimate partners as services are added
or removed \cite{aubet2018graph}. Sensor and actuator series instead describe the physical
process \cite{mathur2016swat}. Network records and process time series require different
feature preparation, while a modular learning interface permits estimators to be exchanged
\cite{iuno_ap4}. RQ1 concerns how these observations can support anomaly
detection and how monitored relations or learning components can be revised. Its separate
studies examine communication graphs and a modular framework for network and process features.

\subsection*{The evaluation problem}

Attack libraries such as Foolbox and earlier attack/defence competitions provide established
evaluation infrastructure \cite{rauber2017foolbox,kurakin2018competition}. Reuse across
prediction tasks requires explicit attacker access and valid perturbation constraints,
together with scoring rules that match the prediction objective \cite{carlini2019evaluating}.
RQ4 examines these requirements through the SAFAIR contest and benchmark. Reusable interfaces
support a common execution procedure. Reproducing a historical result also requires the
evaluated data and trained models, with the complete experimental configuration.

\subsection*{Scope}

The principal empirical studies analyse Dalvik bytecode recovered from Android application
packages; \hybroid{} also uses network traffic recorded during execution. The industrial
studies use observations from an \iuno{} project deployment and the Secure Water Treatment
(SWaT) testbed. These studies observe communication records or process measurements under
their respective collection conditions.

The adversarial-evaluation work concerns facial-image tasks and a partner histopathology
benchmark \cite{sparta2020d72,sparta2022d76}. A short, attributed PDF-malware comparison
illustrates constraints on another input domain \cite{sparta2021d75}. The contribution is
evaluation design and measurement analysis; the evaluated defence algorithms remain the
work of project partners.

The studies are related through their observation and evaluation choices, with separate
datasets and protocols. The IoT work did not motivate \hybroid{}. The Android systems have
not been compared on a common corpus or evaluated here against adaptive attackers.
Chapter~\ref{chap:discussion} makes the conceptual comparison and identifies requirements
for those subsequent experiments.

\section{Research Questions}
\label{sec:intro:rqs}

Four research questions specify the two themes. The first has two subquestions for
networked and industrial settings. The Android questions concern cross-modal and
higher-order representations; the fourth concerns adversarial evaluation.
Section~\ref{sec:background:summary} develops their literature-based motivation as gaps
G1 to G4.

\researchquestion[1]{RQ1}{Behaviour in networked and industrial systems}{\rqOneText}

RQ1 addresses gap~\gapref{1} through service relations and industrial observations.
The two subquestions concern distinct mechanisms and experimental settings.

\researchquestion[1.1]{RQ1.1}{Communication graphs}{\rqOneOneText}

The answer covers node-local relations and operator-approved updates, supported by memory
and latency measurements. Detection accuracy against a labelled attack set was not measured.

\researchquestion[1.2]{RQ1.2}{Modular industrial anomaly detection}{\rqOneTwoText}

The answer concerns a modular interface for network and process features. Supervised
classification on S7-300 records and a multivariate detector on SWaT provide separate
empirical cases.

\researchquestion[2]{RQ2}{Multimodal Android malware analysis}{\rqTwoText}

Gap~\gapref{2} is examined on a common retained cohort of CICAndMal2017 applications.
The contrast is between each modality and their fusion, separately for each learner and
prediction task. The category task includes Benign as one of five classes.

\researchquestion[3]{RQ3}{Higher-order Android malware analysis}{\rqThreeText}

For gap~\gapref{3}, the empirical answer concerns complete configurations: construction and
weighting change together, so an attention-only effect remains unresolved. The tasks are
binary detection and dataset-specific classification: \familyclassification{} on Drebin,
and \categoryclassification{} on CICMalDroid, with Benign as one category.

\researchquestion[4]{RQ4}{Adversarial robustness evaluation}{\rqFourText}

Gap~\gapref{4} is addressed through benchmark design and analysis of the resulting
measurements. The available aggregates support conclusions about scoring and evaluation
requirements, but do not establish a general relation between clean performance and
adversarial robustness.

\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:intro:contributions}

\paragraph{Multimodal Android malware analysis.}
\hybroid{} combines a representation of static program structure with features of recorded
network flows. Its within-system comparison measures the value of fusion on applications
for which both observations are available \cite{norouzian2021hybroid}. Fusion has the
highest reported detection $F_1$ for each evaluated learner. For category classification,
fusion improves $F_1$ for the ensemble classifiers, while the decision tree favours static features.
These are descriptive comparisons of the published fold means
(Section~\ref{sec:hybroid:11}).

\paragraph{Higher-order Android malware analysis.}
\hgann{} groups methods by call neighbourhood and semantic similarity. Slice-aware
hyperedges add relations around security-sensitive API calls, and learned membership
weights control aggregation over the groups \cite{norouzian2025hgannmal}.
The evaluated hypergraph configurations have higher reported accuracy than the pairwise
baselines; \hgann{} exceeds the strongest non-attentive baseline in three of four
task--corpus combinations. The operator analysis in Section~\ref{sec:hgann:8} also shows
how attentive propagation can distinguish groupings with the same fixed propagation
matrix. This representational capacity is separate from an empirical explanation of the
accuracy differences.

\paragraph{Communication-graph modelling for IoT services.}
The service-graph study represents observed communication partners as node-local
relations and permits updates approved by the site owner \cite{aubet2018graph}.
Graph traces and resource measurements document the maintenance of these relations as
services change. The contribution addresses representation and update procedures under
the observation conditions of the middleware.

\paragraph{Modular industrial anomaly detection.}
\nadics{}, Network Anomaly Detection for Industrial Control Systems, separates feature
preparation from interchangeable learners. Its protocol-specific extractor and model
interface support the \iuno{} integration \cite{iuno_ap4,iuno_ap7}. Separate S7-300
evaluations report attack classification and false-alarm episodes during benign operation.
A generative adversarial network (GAN) extension compares representations of multivariate
SWaT process data. Their different recall and false-positive profiles relate the
representation choice to the operating objective (Section~\ref{sec:anomaly:gan}).

\paragraph{Threat-guided evaluation of adversarial defences.}
SAFAIR translates attacker assumptions into a contest protocol and a reusable benchmark
with task-specific interfaces \cite{sparta2021d73,sparta2022d76}. The partner
histopathology application documents reuse outside the contest's facial-image tasks.
Chapter~\ref{chap:advml} derives the zero accuracy loss of constant predictions under
label-preserving attacks and analyses the effect of reversing a binary prediction after
an alarm. These results identify limitations of accuracy-loss ranking and specify the
joint outcomes needed to assess a defended classifier. The contest received no independent
attack submissions, so its delivered results remain distinct from the planned adaptive
competition.

\paragraph{A comparison across the contributions.}
Chapter~\ref{chap:discussion} distinguishes the acquisition of additional observations
from the computational cost of structuring available evidence. Runtime capture supplies
an additional observation to \hybroid{}, whereas \hgann{} constructs its groups from
static information. The discussion relates these choices to the conditions required for
predictive comparison and complete-system adversarial testing, which informs the common-cohort
evaluation proposed as future work.

relational learning, with the network and process concepts needed for

with competing systems remain in the contribution chapters.
